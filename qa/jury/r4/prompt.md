You are an independent juror. You will judge a finished browser game, a GitHub Game Off 2026 entry (theme SHIFT), played by jam participants on desktop for 5–15 minutes. You did not make it.

The game: three additive colour layers (red, green, blue); a "lens" (mouse wheel or Q/E) slides red one way and blue the other while green never moves; the runner stands only on red, green kills, blue is water, and the exit only exists where all three overlap. It is presented as a 1950s patent filing re-cut as a cut-paper film title sequence: each level is a drawing sheet (FIG. 1–10) on a flat colour field; dash-dot phantom outlines with primed numerals preview where the lens will move things before the player commits; the door is numbered 18 when it aligns; a death is circled and stamped ANTICIPATED; Esc opens the "Brief Description of the Drawings" (level select); the end grants Letters Patent with claims written from the player's run.

You get: still frames (file names describe what they show), contact sheets of frame-stepped motion (time-labelled, 0.25 s apart), and, last, contact sheets of three recent Awwwards Sites of the Day (October 2026) as the craft bar for motion. Frame-check facts measured on the motion clip: 50 frame-level events in the 23 s demo, at designed cuts, stamps, paper lifts and the lens-step settle; holds of 433 ms at 7.70 s (ALLOWED before the pull), 500 ms at 11.03 s (death freeze and stamp) and 433 ms at 12.63 s (the stamp held readable while the sheet resets).

Process:
1. One neutral observation per still (what is there).
2. Familiarity audit: list every visible device (palette, type treatment, layout, motion device, effect) and tag each "seen this week" or "no precedent".
3. Swap and removal tests: could these visuals ship unchanged for a different game? What could be deleted without loss?
4. Score each 1–10 with a one-line reason, using these anchors (from Awwwards Sites of the Day, July–Oct 2026): 6.5 competent / Honorable Mention; 7.2–7.6 Site of the Day (median 7.35); 7.7–8.0 Site of the Month contender; 8.1+ Site of the Year territory; 9 almost never. For any 8+, name the thing that would stop a juror who has seen a thousand sites.
   - Design (hierarchy per frame, type craft, colour discipline, composition, consistency across sheets, details)
   - Usability (what to do within 10 s, the lens and the carried/not-carried rule legible before committing, readability over everything, wayfinding)
   - Creativity (one ownable idea that shapes the interface, the signature moment, at least one no-precedent device)
   - Content (real, specific copy; one coherent image treatment; no placeholders)
   - Craft in motion (against the reference sheets: does every input get an answer, do transitions and the image feel like one material, are effects tuned from the subject or at defaults)
   Then the jam's own categories, 1–5 stars as itch.io raters would give: Graphics, Audio (judge only from what the stills and descriptions imply; say if you can't), Innovation, Theme interpretation (SHIFT), Overall.
5. Top fixes, at most 7, each with exact values (sizes, durations, colours, positions), and a keep list of what must not be lost.
