### 3. Swap and Removal Tests

* **Swap Test (Could these visuals ship unchanged for a different game?):** **No.** Every graphical element is structurally bound to the two core mechanics: (1) the directional hatching (`|` on red, `/` on green, `—` on blue) and white union (`18`) directly encode the three-layer additive collision rules, and (2) the patent drafting conventions (primed phantom outlines `10'`/`12'`, airborne clearance bars `I`, and prior-art trajectory citations on death) exist specifically to make the coupled-on-ground vs. uncoupled-in-air lens shift predictable before and after the player acts. Put this skin on a generic platformer and half the notation (`10'`, `12'`, `20/22/24`, `I`, triple-layer door `18`) becomes non-functional decoration.
* **Removal Test (What could be deleted without loss?):**
  1. **The persistent dial (`20`) and shift-direction arrows (`22`, `24`) during normal running** — on horizontal levels (`FIG. 1`, `FIG. 2`), the phantom outlines (`10'`, `12'`) and the RGB door strips already show exact lens offset and direction on the playfield itself; the left-margin dial and arrows only earn their keep when axis orientation changes in `FIG. 6` (vertical shift) or when called out on death.
  2. **The continuous footstep arc trail (`10'`) behind the runner during simple horizontal walking (`5.50 s–7.67 s` in `FIG. 1`)** — once the lens stops moving, trailing the runner's walk with a dashed arc adds visual noise near the `12` leader line without aiding puzzle foresight (unlike the full death trajectory at `12.17 s`, which is diagnostic).

* **Creativity: 8.5 / 10** — Reframing an RGB phase-shift puzzle as an examined patent application—where alternative lens positions are "alternative embodiments" (`12'`) and dying means your attempt was "anticipated by prior art"—is a wholly original conceptual synthesis that governs mechanics, UI, and narrative simultaneously.
