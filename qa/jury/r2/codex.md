**3. Swap and removal tests**

The paper treatment, display lettering, coloured backgrounds and oversized numbers could accommodate another puzzle game with relatively few changes. Those elements alone would not make this ownable.

The opposing channel indicators, carried-player predictions, three-part exit and run-specific claims could not ship unchanged around different mechanics. They describe this game’s actual rules. The strongest connection is the ending: the filing becomes a record of how the player solved it.

What could disappear with little loss:

- The fixed HUD references **20, 22 and 24**, unless another visible page uses them as an index. Direction and lens value already carry the practical information.
- Duplicate callouts where the object and its ghost are already clear.
- The secondary shadow or echo on small prose. The surrounding objects establish the material.
- Some repeated transition time after the first few sheets.

Removing the primed previews, attempt citations or personalised claims would substantially weaken the work.

**5. Top fixes**

Proposed values below use the **1440 × 900 desktop frame** as the reference size.

1. **Keep the carrying rule available during play.** Add a two-line state label at **x24, y710**, using **22 px type / 26 px leading**: “ON RED / CARRIED” or “IN AIR / NOT CARRIED.” Use ink on orange and cream on slate. Update it with the physical state.

2. **Resolve annotation collisions before adding detail.** Maintain **12 px clearance** between labels and object silhouettes, and **16 px between labels**. Give leaders a **24 px minimum length**. In FIG. 9, move “10” above-right of the runner; in FIG. 6, move it clear of the platform edge. Preserve a **28 px minimum annotation size**.

3. **Separate exit fragments from terrain.** FIG. 9’s hatching competes with the fragments, while blue loses strength against slate in FIG. 6. Give exit fragments a **3 px background-coloured clearance**; on slate, add a **2 px cream edge, #F1E9D2**. Preserve their existing coloured fills and directional hatching.

4. **Retain a compact control reminder.** Place “Q / E OR WHEEL · LENS     A / D · MOVE     W · JUMP     ESC · SHEETS” at **x228, y844**, in **20 px type**. The cover should not be the only place a returning player can recover the controls.

5. **Make small prose crisper and the run facts easier to find.** Set index and claim copy to **18 px / 25 px**, with **zero offset shadow**. Underline only variable facts—such as “3.4 seconds” or “four prior attempts”—with a **1 px rule, 3 px below the baseline**. Keep the full patent wording.

6. **Shorten repeated sheet handoffs while preserving the holds.** After the first introduction, target **650 ms** for the handoff: **250 ms paper removal + 400 ms numeral settling**. Keep the demonstrated **433 ms success hold** and **533 ms death freeze** separate from that budget.

**Keep:** the RGB overlap becoming door 18; colour-specific hatching; primed predictions of player and platform movement; the carried/not-carried demonstration; the numeral becoming the HUD; circled deaths and ANTICIPATED citations; restrained paper texture; and, above all, claims written from the player’s run.
