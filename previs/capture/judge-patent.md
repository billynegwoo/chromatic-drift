You are an independent juror judging a greybox previs (primitives only, no art yet) of a presentation concept for "Chromatic Drift", a browser puzzle platformer for a game jam (played 5–15 minutes on desktop). The world is three additive colour layers; the "lens" (mouse wheel or Q/E) slides red one way and blue the other while green never moves; the runner stands only on red, green kills, blue is water, and the exit exists only where all three overlap. Standing on red, the lens carries you; in mid-air it doesn't.

Concept, one sentence: the game is an examiner's photostat of a 1950s patent filing, one drawing sheet per level, and the lens is shown the way patent drawings show moving parts: the next lens position drawn as dash-dot phantom outlines with primed numerals (12', 16', and 10' for the runner when it will be carried), so the rule can be read off the drawing before the player commits; the door is unlabelled until aligned, when a leader line draws in and "18" is typed; a death is circled in red pencil and stamped ANTICIPATED.

The capture is a scripted 10-second demo in virtual time: level 1 (turn the lens twice, the door aligns and gets its number, walk in), then level 2 (a mid-air lens turn across a gap, then standing still while turning the lens carries the runner into spikes: a death).

Answer each, with the frame times you base it on:
1. Is the mechanic (what the lens will do, and the carried/not-carried rule) legible within 10 seconds without instructions?
2. Is it ownable, or does it look like a known effect or another game?
3. Does it carry the game's structure (levels, alignment, death, progression) or only decorate it?
4. Any motion faults you can see (pop-ins, stalls, unclear moments)? Phantoms popping in/out and hard level cuts are expected in a greybox; say whether they hurt legibility.
5. Verdict: PASS (develop it), RESHAPE (say exactly what), or KILL. Then one paragraph on the single biggest risk if it goes to production.
