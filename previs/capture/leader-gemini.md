### 2. Ownability vs. Known Effects or Other Games

* **The puzzle mechanic alone is familiar; the 35mm film-transport integration makes it ownable.**
  * In isolation, RGB colour-layer platforming and chromatic-aberration alignment (`6.50 s – 7.33 s`, `10.50 s – 10.67 s`, `13.33 s – 13.50 s`) evoke familiar colour-channel puzzle platformers (*Hue*, jam games built around RGB split shaders). Inside the level shots (`6.50 s – 11.50 s` and `13.33 s – 14.83 s`), the red brick tiles, green triangle spikes (`13.33 s`), and rectangular door frames sitting on a dark grid look like a conventional retro puzzle-platformer.
  * What makes the presentation genuinely ownable is grounding RGB translation in **physical film registration inside a 4-perf 35mm gate**: the persistent perforated film border with edge-print metadata (`CD-01-LEADER 8-2`, `35 · 4 PERF · 24 FPS` from `0.00 s`), playable SMPTE countdown numerals (`8..2` at `0.00 s – 6.17 s` and `4..2` at `11.83 s – 13.17 s`), blue sprocket-band water (`0.00 s – 6.17 s`), green emulsion scratch line, hatched splice block (`4.50 s – 6.17 s`, clip `00:27–00:37`), and the one-frame white sync-pop ring on numeral `2` (`6.33 s` and `13.17 s`, clip `00:38` and `01:19`) before the frame advances vertically into the gate.

### 3. Does It Carry the Game's Structure or Only Decorate It?

* **It carries the structure in the leaders, goal state, and HUD, though the trimmed leader and level interiors slip toward decoration:**
  * **Progression & Transitions:** The countdown leader is not a passive cutscene; it is built from the game's exact collision layers (red numerals, blue sprocket water below, green gate scratch above) and uses landing on numeral `2` (`6.17 s – 6.33 s` and `13.17 s`, clip `00:37–00:38` and `01:19`) as the physical trigger that pops and advances the next shot into the gate (`6.33 s – 6.50 s` and `11.67 s – 11.83 s`).
  * **Alignment Goal:** Additive colour registration (`Red + Green + Blue = White`) directly binds the lens input to opening the exit door (`6.67 s – 7.33 s`, clip `00:40–00:44`) and clearing the shot (`ALIGNED` at `10.50 s`, clip `01:03`).
  * **Diegetic Metadata:** The top-left edge print updates structurally across the reel (`CD-01-LEADER 8-2` at `0.00 s` $\rightarrow$ `CD-01-TK01` at `6.50 s` $\rightarrow$ `CD-02-LEADER 4-2` at `11.83 s` $\rightarrow$ `CD-02-TK01` at `13.33 s`), encoding world, shot, and take number directly into the film stock.
  * **Where it under-carries:**
    1. **Trimmed Leader (`11.83 s – 13.17 s`, clip `01:11–01:19`):** The `4-3-2` leader between levels has narrow step gaps requiring zero lens interaction (`"hold right and land on the 2"`), reducing a structural mechanic to a 1.3-second walk-through buffer.
    2. **Unseen Death / Re-rack Loop:** Because `deaths 0` never increments and `TK01` never advances to `TK02` during the `14.83 s` capture, the projector loop-loss and re-rack death mechanic is structurally set up by the edge print but never proven in motion.

### 4. Motion Faults and Their Impact on Legibility

1. **Camera scroll masks the mid-air lens shift (`1.67 s – 2.33 s`, clip `00:10–00:14`):** Turning the lens mid-air (`1.83 s – 2.17 s`) occurs while the viewport is actively panning right to follow the runner across the leader. Without a stationary vertical green landmark in the playfield, the red layer's leftward lens motion and the camera's rightward follow-scroll cancel visually. **Hurts legibility** of the mid-air decoupling rule.
2. **Edge-to-edge red floor conceals the standing carry (`6.67 s – 7.33 s`, clip `00:40–00:44`):** Because the red brick platform in Level 1 spans the full width of the gate, shifting the red layer left only moves internal brick seams and drifts the idle runner ~1.1 grid squares left while the player is watching the doors align on the right. **Hurts legibility** of the standing-carry rule.
3. **Empty incoming frames and missing roll on frame-advance transitions (`6.33 s – 6.50 s`, clip `00:38–00:39`; `11.67 s – 11.83 s`, clip `01:10–01:11`; `13.17 s – 13.33 s`, clip `01:19–01:20`):**
   * During the vertical gate rolls at `6.33 s` (clip `00:38`) and `11.67 s` (clip `01:10`), the incoming shot enters the bottom of the gate **empty** (showing only the background grid and HUD text `1/10 LENS` or `LEADER -> 2/10 LEAP`), and its red platforms, doors, and runner pop in on the very next frame (`6.50 s` and `11.83 s`).
   * At `13.17 s – 13.33 s` (clip `01:19–01:20`), the vertical roll frame is skipped altogether: at `13.17 s` (clip `01:19`), the sync-pop ring appears on numeral `2` and the bottom text prematurely swaps to Level 2's prompt, followed by a hard cut into `CD-02-TK01` at `13.33 s` (clip `01:20`).
   * **Impact:** Even allowing for greybox cuts, scrolling an empty frame into the gate before popping level geometry in undermines the physical readability of a continuous film strip where adjacent frames are pre-printed on the reel.
4. **One-second post-exit stall (`10.50 s – 11.50 s`, clip `01:03–01:09`):** After reaching the aligned white door at `10.50 s` (clip `01:03`), the runner freezes in place for `1.00 s` of real time while `ALIGNED` sits static on screen before the reel advances at `11.67 s` (clip `01:10`). Compared to the snappy ~0.17 s pop-and-advance on numeral `2` (`6.17 s – 6.33 s`), this pause stalls the reel's rhythm.
5. **Runner jump trail / phantom afterimages (`0.67 s – 6.17 s`, clip `00:04–00:37`; `12.00 s – 13.17 s`, clip `01:12–01:19`):** Faint red ghost squares trail the runner's jump arcs. This **does not hurt legibility** (it slightly clarifies jump trajectory), though it feels more like digital persistence than discrete 24 fps celluloid frames.

### 5. Verdict: **RESHAPE**

**What to reshape before production:**
1. **Make Level 1 (`6.50 s – 11.50 s`) visually teach the standing-carry rule without text:** Replace the full-width red brick floor with an isolated red island platform separated from a second platform by a gap or stationary green marker, so when the player turns the lens at `6.67 s – 7.33 s`, they unmistakably see the red platform carry their character across the screen relative to stationary green geometry.
2. **Anchor the mid-air lens shift (`1.83 s – 2.17 s`) against a stationary green landmark:** In the head leader, drop a visible green gate-scratch tick or registration crosshair into the vertical space between numerals `7` and `6` (and lock or slow camera follow during lens rotation) so the player can clearly see their airborne runner hold position against the green reference while the red numeral `6` slides underneath them.
3. **Pre-populate incoming frames during gate advances (`6.33 s`, `11.67 s`, `13.17 s`) and trim the `ALIGNED` freeze (`10.50 s – 11.50 s`):** Render the incoming shot's actual geometry inside the rising frame during the vertical pull-down transition rather than popping it in after the roll, and cut the post-door `ALIGNED` hold from `1.00 s` down to ~`0.25 s – 0.35 s` so the reel keeps its 24 fps mechanical snap.

**Single biggest risk if it goes to production:**
The biggest production risk is **input and spatial-tracking overload when mid-air platforming collides with continuous lens scrolling**. In a 5–15 minute desktop jam game, asking players to simultaneously jump and steer (`W/A/D` or arrows) while scrolling or tapping the lens (`Q/E` or mouse wheel) to shift red platforms one way and blue water the exact opposite way against stationary green kill-hazards (`13.33 s`, clip `01:20`) can easily turn intentional registration puzzles into fiddly mid-air trial-and-error—especially if the camera ever scrolls or if lens offsets use fine incremental ticks rather than bold, readable detents. If the lens is not constrained to a small number of snappy, high-contrast registration stops (with strong stationary green landmarks in every shot so the player can judge where red and blue will land without looking up at the tiny top-center HUD at `1.83 s` or `6.67 s`), players will fight the controls rather than enjoy the film-machine fantasy.
